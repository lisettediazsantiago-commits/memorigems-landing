What is a Gem?

A voice, and whatever helps it land.

  • A voice recordingFirst, and the only part that isn't optional. It's what a photograph can't carry.
  • A photo, if you have oneOne picture that means something. Not an album — and not required.
  • A captionA sentence or two. As much as you feel like saying.

Together they become one Gem — a small moment, kept. Not a biography. Not a timeline. Just the moment.

Gem Keepers Circle

One Gem Keeper.
A circle of Gem Makers.

The Gem Keeper is the person who receives — the one living with memory change. The Gem Makers are everyone who records for them. You decide who is in the circle, and how far in each of them comes.

  • StewardThe account holder. Sets up the profile and decides who is in the circle.
  • Co-stewardEverything you can do — invite others, manage sharing. A trusted second.
  • ContributorRecord and send Gems, add updates. Can't change sharing or the circle.
  • ViewerListen to Gems and see the profile. A gentle way in for distant family.

Accessibility

Everyone deserves connection.

Not everyone reads small print, holds a steady hand, or has the same first language. So MemoriGems was built for the person who finds screens hardest — and it turns out that makes it easier for everybody.

  • Large, adjustable text
  • A voice-first experience
  • Pages that read themselves aloud
  • Screen reader support
  • Plain language throughout
  • Nothing for them to install
  • One thing on screen at a time
  • Nothing that can be got wrong

Questions families ask

The things worth knowing first.

If what you need isn’t here, write to us. A person reads it.

I don’t know what to say.

Nobody does at first. Comfort and Routine Gems offer prompts — tap New suggestions for more, or type a word and tap Ideas. Thirty seconds is plenty. “I was thinking about you” is a whole Gem.

Does the person receiving it need an app, or an account?

No. A Gem arrives as a link they can open on any phone or computer. On a tablet you set it up once with a code — no password, nothing for them to remember, nothing for them to install.

Will it interrupt them?

Nothing buzzes at them. There is no alert and no email — a Gem waits until the next time they open MemoriGems. That is deliberate: a device that pings at someone with memory loss creates more confusion than it solves.

What if they don’t remember who I am?

That is often the moment MemoriGems is for. Names and faces can go before voices do — families tell us the sound of someone familiar lands when a photograph on its own does not. It does not fix anything. It gives you something to offer on a day when you have run out of things to say.

Can they send anything back?

They can send a heart, and you will see it on their page. Hearts are deliberately the only thing — it is the one gesture that stays easy when a lot of other things get hard.

Who else can see this?

Only the people in their circle, and anyone you have sent a Connection Snapshot link to. Both are listed on their page, and both can be changed or switched off at any time.

What do you record about how it is used?

When a Gem is played and when a heart is sent — so you can see whether your Gems are landing. Nothing is recorded about what anyone says or does otherwise, and none of it is shared outside your family.

Are the recordings ours to keep?

Yes. At the end of the beta we prepare a folder of everything — the audio and photos as real files, with a page that plays them on any computer, with no account and no internet. Yours, for as long as you keep it.

Is this only for families facing dementia?

It was built for memory change, and that is where it is being tested first. But nothing in it is only for that — a voice worth keeping is a voice worth keeping, whether the distance is illness, miles, or time.

What does it cost?

Nothing during the founding families pilot. What it costs afterwards has not been decided yet — and we would rather tell you that than put up a number we are not ready to stand behind. Founding families will hear it from us first.

Talk to a person

Anything at all — something broken, something confusing, or a hard week. We’d rather hear it than not. help@huluxinnovations.com, read by the people who built this.

If you’re the one having a hard time, the Alzheimer’s Association runs a free helpline 24 hours a day for families and caregivers — 800‑272‑3900. They are better at that than we are, and there’s no wrong reason to call.
